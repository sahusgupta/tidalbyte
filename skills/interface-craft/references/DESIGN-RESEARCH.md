## Synthesis
The problem is not any one gradient, font, or card. The problem is a *context-free default*: the same visual formula appears for unrelated industries and workflows. The remedy is subject-specific art direction, actual content, a deliberately unusual but functional focal composition, consistent typography, and repeated screenshot critique.

Physical materials and oversized subjects are strong preferences for expressive surfaces, not mandatory everywhere. For a dense productivity app, precision, hierarchy, speed, and native behavior take precedence over visual spectacle.

## Useful mental tests
- **Logo swap:** Could another company's logo be substituted without changing the page? If yes, identity is weak.
- **Material test:** Can you explain why this surface looks like paper, plywood, metal, or fabric? If no, it is decoration.
- **Crop test:** Is the oversized element cropped to emphasize its identity, or simply cut off?
- **10-second test:** Can a new user identify the product and primary action immediately?
- **Removal test:** If a flourish disappears, does the screen lose meaning or only noise?
- **Platform test:** Does each device feel native to its interaction model while retaining the same brand identity?
